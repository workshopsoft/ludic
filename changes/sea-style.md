**The open sea.** A still-water plane in sea style (`sea: true` on a `WaterBodies` row, or `water_sea_add`): the surface stays flat and seven long swells bend the light, each fading out as its wavelength comes within a few pixels of the screen's so none crawls as glitter, and the whole flat toward the horizon; the colour deepens with the water under it and is plain deep sea from nine metres down and over the edge of a map's ground; a foam band lies along every shore where the water over the bed is under a metre deep (white at the swash, lace out to the band's edge, nothing at a metre, none on the dry texels of the wet mask, none where the plane meets the ground to the millimetre), on the sea only. The rectangle reaches `WATER_SEA_REACH` (100 km, or the camera's far distance if more) past the map, so the sea runs to the horizon from anywhere on it. Drawn on the wet mask's wet texels with no reflection pass (the sky is read from the prefiltered sky), one quad: on the lab's coast the pass costs about 1.1 ms at the beach and 0.85 at the horizon (1.4 with the sea filling the frame) on an M4 Pro at 1080p, against 2.2, 2.0 and 4.4 for the same plane in the old style (`R3D_PROF=1`, `lab/sea_lab.ludic`). The sea reads its tiles' depth only where the bed is shallower than nine metres. A lake or a river keeps its look, and its thin wash can be switched off with `wash: false` (`water_body_wash`). A mesh body (a river mouth) drawn at the sea's height now wins over the plane instead of fighting it: planes are drawn a hair behind. The wet mask is read across tile borders (four texels each through the page) instead of clamped inside a tile's layer, so a border is not a step of half a texel in the edge. `prof_reset` starts the GPU means again for a lab holding one view then the next, and the water pass's scene capture has its own slot (`water capture`).
