Three times cheaper than the first sea (68 %, 63 % and 61 % off).
Where it went, in the order it was done (open sea; ms each, measured by stopping the shader at each step and by taking each piece out):
  the swell: seven trigonometric swells a pixel (0.32 ms, and the striping) -> two reads of one baked tile and one of another (0.05)
  the sky: four fetches for the reflection (up and ground, two levels each) and four for the haze, and an atan and acos each -> one fetch
           of the nearest level, serving both, and polynomial arc functions (0.2 -> 0.1)
  the shadow: a cascade tap, the terrain's and the clouds' reads a pixel -> only where the sun's glitter or the foam is there to be lit (0.13 -> 0)
  the bed: refraction, six depth reads and a blurred read -> under four metres of water one read of the scene's mips, and none at all
           where the bed cannot come through (under a hundredth)
  the program: the sea's code alone in its own program (WATER_SEA), the lake's in the other: 20 % off, from the registers the other's
           branches held
  the foam: the lace's noise is not computed where a pixel could not show it
Not worth doing (measured, none moved a view by more than the noise): a flat reflection direction, one coarse read instead of two,
half precision for the shading, a box round the wet mask's non-wet tiles to skip its read outside it (it is in the history of this work, not
in the code). What is left is a floor of 0.11-0.13 ms (raster, the colour and depth writes of 2 M pixels on this machine's bandwidth) and
about twenty small pieces of 0.01-0.08 ms each.

THE BUDGET. The contract's line is "at most 1.0 ms GPU at 1080p on M1". It was NOT run on an M1 (none here). An M1 draws these views about
3 times slower than this machine (8 GPU cores against 16, the clock and the memory bandwidth, 68 GB/s against 273, up with them), so the
same line is 1.0 / 3 = 0.33 ms here, and the pass against it is:
  horizon (the camera at the water's edge looking out, half the frame sea)   0.27-0.31   meets it  (about 0.8-0.9 ms on an M1)
  land (the plane under the land the camera faces)                           0.17-0.19   meets it
  coast                                                                      0.45-0.50   over it by 35-50 %  (about 1.4-1.5 ms on an M1)
  open sea, the sea filling the whole frame                                  0.47-0.51   over it by 40-50 %  (about 1.4-1.5 ms on an M1)
The floor alone (writing colour and depth for 2 M pixels, a shader that does nothing) is 0.11-0.13 ms here, an estimated 0.35-0.4 ms on
an M1: a sea that fills the frame leaves 0.6 ms of an M1's 1.0 for everything else. So the line cannot be kept for a full-frame sea or a
foam-filled beach at 1080p with the features the contract asks for (swell, depth colour, foam band, a horizon at the far plane), and is
kept for the views a player has most of the time. This is for the owner to settle by amending OC-39's budget line in
docs/tech/mvp-contracts.md and docs/plan/decisions.md, not left as 'deferred'; the text proposed is at the end of this file.
lab/sea_budget.py is the guard until then. It FAILS when the pass costs more than a share of the old still-water plane's time in the same
view (0.35 open sea and horizon, 0.40 coast, 0.65 land), the two run interleaved in one session so the machine's load (other jobs share
this GPU: the same pass read 0.45 and 0.70 ms on the coast, an absolute ceiling of 0.60 ms failed an unchanged shader) is the same to
both; the first sea (1.46, 0.74, 1.17 ms) is over every share. Milliseconds are printed against the scaled line and never fail it.
lab/sea_budget_test.py tests the guard (shipped pass in, load-scaled pass in, first sea out, a view that did not report out).
EVERY M1 FIGURE IN THIS FILE IS AN ESTIMATE (this machine's time times 3); no M1 run exists. At HEAD bf1d4667, M4 Pro, least of 4, loaded
machine: coast 0.45, horizon 0.27, open sea 0.49, land 0.17, deep bed 0.30-0.58 ms.
Tried and not kept: a second sea program for rectangles that are wet at every pixel (no mask read, no discard), a baked foam-lace field
instead of the fbm, a cheaper fog pow and one wet-mask read for the four foam taps. Together they read 0.31 (coast), 0.18 (horizon), 0.31
(open sea) on a loaded machine, but the baked lace drew crisp cellular blotches instead of the soft lace (flick_a shot) and failed
sea_check's flicker line (0.1 % of pixels moved by up to 55/255 between frames, 19 against a limit of 14, head 11); with the fbm put
back the split alone measured 0.52 / 0.25 / 0.50, no gain over HEAD within the load's noise. The work was dropped uncommitted, not
shipped half-measured; the next saving to try is a lace field that matches the fbm's soft edge, with sea_check's flicker line as its test.
