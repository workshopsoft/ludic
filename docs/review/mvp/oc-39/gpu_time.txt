Where it went, in the order it was done (open sea; ms each, measured by stopping the shader at each step and by taking each piece out):
  the swell: seven trigonometric swells a pixel (0.32 ms, and the striping) -> two reads of one baked tile and one of another (0.05)
  the sky: four fetches for the reflection (up and ground, two levels each) and four for the haze, and an atan and acos each -> one fetch
           of the nearest level, serving both, and polynomial arc functions (0.2 -> 0.1)
  the shadow: a cascade tap, the terrain's and the clouds' reads a pixel -> only where the sun's glitter or the foam is there to be lit (0.13 -> 0)
  the bed: refraction, six depth reads and a blurred read -> under four metres of water one read of the scene's mips, and none at all
           where the bed cannot come through (under a hundredth)
  the program: the sea's code alone in its own program (WATER_SEA), the lake's in the other: 20 % off, from the registers the other's
           branches held
  the foam: the lace's noise is not computed where a pixel could not show it
Not worth doing (measured, none moved a view by more than the noise): a flat reflection direction, one coarse read instead of two,
half precision for the shading, a box round the wet mask's non-wet tiles to skip its read outside it (it is in the history of this work, not
in the code). What is left is a floor of 0.11-0.13 ms (raster, the colour and depth writes of 2 M pixels on this machine's bandwidth) and
about twenty small pieces of 0.01-0.08 ms each.
